# Art Tower Wars — Plain Language Glossary

## Logical Gameplay Viewport

The fixed gameplay area the game is designed around.

Think of this as:

* the “camera window”
* the gameplay rectangle
* the competitive play area

This area should always show:

* battlefield
* towers
* creeps
* deployment UI
* timers/resources

Example:
You might decide:

* all gameplay is designed around a 1080x1920 portrait rectangle

Even if a phone is physically larger or wider, gameplay still fits inside this same logical area.

Why it matters:
Without this, different devices may:

* crop gameplay
* stretch gameplay
* hide UI
* show more battlefield to some players

---

## Gameplay Footprint

The actual gameplay size of an object.

This is NOT art size.

It means:
“How much gameplay space does this object occupy?”

Used for:

* collision
* targeting
* spacing
* lane blocking
* balance

Example:
A creep may visually look huge,
but still only occupy:

* 48x48 gameplay units

Another creep may look tiny,
but occupy the SAME gameplay footprint.

Why it matters:
Gameplay fairness must remain consistent even when skins differ.

---

## Gameplay Render Box

The visual area the art is allowed to occupy onscreen.

This is the “art container.”

Example:

Gameplay footprint:
48x48

Allowed visual render box:
64x64

Meaning:

* gameplay collision stays 48x48
* art can visually extend beyond that slightly

This allows:

* hats
* weapons
* wings
* decorative shapes

without changing gameplay.

---

## Source Resolution

The resolution the artist originally creates the art in.

Examples:

* 32x32 pixel art
* 512x512 HD illustration

This is ONLY production quality.

It should NOT affect:

* gameplay balance
* collision
* onscreen size

Two artists may create:

* different source resolutions
* different art styles

but both export into:

* the same render box
* the same gameplay size

---

## Sprite

A 2D image used in-game.

Examples:

* creep art
* tower art
* projectile art
* explosion frames

A sprite may be:

* static
* animated
* part of a sprite sheet

---

## Sprite Sheet

A single image containing multiple animation frames.

Example:
A 4-frame walking creep:

Frame 1 | Frame 2 | Frame 3 | Frame 4

The game cycles through frames to create motion.

---

## Frame

One individual image in an animation.

Example:
A 4-frame walk cycle has:

* 4 separate images

More frames:

* smoother animation
* larger file sizes
* more artist work

Fewer frames:

* simpler production
* lighter performance cost

---

## Silhouette

The recognizable outer shape of a character/object.

Good silhouettes are:

* readable instantly
* recognizable at small sizes

Example:
You should identify:

* “that’s a tank”
* “that’s a fast creep”
* “that’s a tower”

even from far away.

This is EXTREMELY important on phones.

---

## Anchor Point

The specific point the game treats as the object’s position.

Usually:

* bottom-center
  OR
* center-center

Used for:

* placement
* movement
* projectile spawning
* collision

Example:
A creep’s feet may always align to the anchor point.

---

## Hitbox / Collision Box

The invisible gameplay area used for collision detection.

Usually simpler than the art itself.

Example:
A dragon sprite may have huge wings,
but the hitbox only covers the body.

This improves:

* fairness
* readability
* consistency

---

## Safe Area

The area guaranteed visible on all devices.

Contains important gameplay elements:

* battlefield
* cards
* resources
* timers

Should NEVER be cut off.

---

## Bleed Area

Extra decorative area outside the safe area.

Can safely:

* expand
* crop
* stretch slightly

Examples:

* background scenery
* clouds
* borders
* decorative lighting

Should NOT contain important gameplay information.

---

## Responsive Layout

A layout that changes based on screen shape/size.

Example:
UI elements may:

* reposition
* resize
* stack differently

Good for:

* menus
* settings
* shop screens

Dangerous for:

* competitive battlefield gameplay

---

## Aspect Ratio

The shape of the screen.

Example:

* 9:16 portrait
* 16:9 landscape

Different phones have different aspect ratios.

This is one reason safe areas matter.

---

## Pixel Art

Artwork intentionally built from visible pixels.

Characteristics:

* sharp edges
* low-resolution source art
* usually nearest-neighbor scaling

Example:
32x32 sprites.

---

## HD Art

Higher-resolution artwork with smoother detail.

Characteristics:

* anti-aliased edges
* painted/detail-heavy appearance
* often larger source files

Example:
512x512 painted character art.

---

## Anti-Aliasing

A smoothing technique that softens jagged edges.

HD art usually uses anti-aliasing.

Pixel art usually avoids it.

Example:
Without anti-aliasing:

* diagonal edges look stair-stepped

With anti-aliasing:

* edges appear smoother

---

## Edge Behavior

How sprite edges visually behave when scaled or displayed.

Pixel art:

* sharp hard edges

HD art:

* soft blended edges

Mixing these carelessly can make a game feel visually inconsistent.

---

## Nearest-Neighbor Scaling

A scaling method commonly used for pixel art.

Keeps pixels:

* sharp
* blocky
* crisp

Without this:
pixel art becomes blurry.

---

## UI (User Interface)

The non-gameplay visual controls/information.

Examples:

* buttons
* mana/resource bars
* timers
* menus
* card rows

---

## VFX (Visual Effects)

Temporary animated visual effects.

Examples:

* explosions
* tower shots
* sparkles
* smoke
* hit flashes

---

## Visual Readability

How quickly players understand what they’re seeing.

Critical for mobile games because:

* screens are small
* matches are fast
* players glance quickly

Good readability usually means:

* clear silhouettes
* strong contrast
* limited clutter
* consistent scale

---

## Visual Density

How visually “busy” something appears.

Too much density:

* cluttered gameplay
* difficult targeting
* player confusion

Competitive mobile games generally benefit from:

* lower visual density
* stronger clarity

---

## Canonical

The official standard/reference version.

Example:
The canonical battlefield layout means:

* the one true approved battlefield reference

Everything else should follow it.

---

## Unified Art Style Per Match

A rule where all battlefield assets in a match use the same visual style.

Example:
If the player selects:

* pixel battlefield skin

then:

* towers
* creeps
* effects
* battlefield

all use pixel style together.

This prevents visual chaos.
